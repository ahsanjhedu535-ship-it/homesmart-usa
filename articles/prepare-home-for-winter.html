Home Maintenance

How to Prepare Your Home for Winter

Prepare for cold weather with draft checks, pipe protection, heating maintenance, alarm testing, drainage observations, and emergency supplies.

Winter preparation looks different in a mild coastal area than in a region with prolonged freezes and snow. The goal is to reduce preventable damage, keep heating equipment operating safely, and make sure your household is ready for interruptions. Use local weather guidance and equipment instructions to adapt this checklist to your home.

Check drafts and weatherstripping

Inspect doors, windows, and accessible attic hatches for obvious gaps. Replace worn weatherstripping and use suitable sealants only where appropriate. Avoid blocking intentional ventilation openings or sealing around combustion equipment without professional guidance. Heavy curtains can help manage window comfort, but they should not cover heaters or create a fire hazard.

Protect vulnerable plumbing

Know where the main water shutoff is and how to reach it. Identify pipes in unheated garages, crawlspaces, basements, or exterior walls. Follow local guidance for insulating vulnerable pipes and managing outdoor faucets before freezing weather. If you will be away during a freeze, seek advice appropriate to your plumbing system and local conditions. If a pipe freezes or bursts, avoid unsafe heating methods and call a qualified plumber when needed.

Have heating equipment checked

Follow the manufacturer's maintenance schedule for furnaces, boilers, heat pumps, and other heating systems. Replace or clean filters as directed, keep vents clear, and address unusual sounds or odors promptly. Do not use an oven or outdoor appliance to heat indoor space. If you use fuel-burning equipment, maintain appropriate alarms and have the system inspected by a qualified professional.

Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

Test alarms according to their instructions and check replacement dates. Install alarms in locations recommended by local codes and the manufacturer. Carbon monoxide is odorless; a functioning alarm is an important safety layer, not a substitute for proper equipment maintenance. If an alarm sounds, move outdoors to fresh air and contact emergency services or the appropriate local responder.

Prepare outdoor faucets, gutters, and drainage

Disconnect hoses and follow local procedures for outdoor faucets. Check gutters and downspouts from a safe location and arrange professional cleaning if access is high or slippery. Make sure drainage carries water away from the foundation. Do not climb onto icy roofs or ladders. After a storm, observe rooflines from the ground and seek professional inspection if you see signs of damage.

Plan for storms and outages

Keep flashlights, batteries, warm clothing, basic first-aid supplies, drinking water, and needed medicines accessible. Charge essential devices when severe weather is expected and understand how to receive local alerts. If you use a generator, operate it outdoors and well away from doors, windows, and vents, following the manufacturer's instructions. Never run a generator, grill, or camp stove inside a home, garage, or enclosed porch.

Manage indoor moisture

Cold weather can increase condensation on windows and cold surfaces. Use kitchen and bathroom exhaust fans as appropriate and watch for recurring dampness. Avoid blocking ventilation. If you find persistent moisture, investigate the source rather than simply painting over it. Proper ventilation and moisture control help protect building materials and indoor air quality.

Adapt to your region

In cold regions, freeze protection and snow-load awareness are important. In milder climates, heavy rain, wind, and temperature swings may be the main concerns. Coastal homes may need storm preparation, while desert homes may have different heating and plumbing considerations. Follow local emergency management guidance and keep important home-system instructions accessible.

Budget for seasonal preparation

Make a list of filter replacements, servicing, weatherstripping, drainage work, and any professional inspections. Use the HomeSmart maintenance planner to estimate a monthly savings target for known seasonal costs and reserve funds for unexpected repairs. This is a planning aid, not a prediction of what your house will cost.

Frequently asked questions

Should I leave the heat on when away?

The safe setting depends on local temperatures, plumbing, home construction, and system type. Follow local guidance and your insurer or property manager's requirements.

Can I use a gas oven to heat my home?

No. Ovens and outdoor combustion appliances should not be used as indoor space heaters because of fire and carbon monoxide risks.

When should I call a professional?

Use qualified help for heating-system faults, roof access, plumbing freeze damage, electrical issues, or work that requires permits or specialized tools.
