Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Home Improvement Contractor

Compare contractor bids, check licensing and insurance, understand permits and warranties, set payment milestones, and document changes before work begins.

HomeSmart USA Editorial Guide · Educational information · Updated October 2026
Photo: Unsplash. Image shown for editorial illustration.

Hiring a contractor is not just about finding the lowest price. A successful project depends on a clear scope, appropriate qualifications, a written agreement, realistic timing, and a plan for changes. Requirements differ by state and locality, so use this guide to prepare questions and verify the rules that apply to your specific project.

A practical note: Your home, local climate, utility rules, and contractor scope all affect the right decision. Treat estimates as planning scenarios and verify important details with official sources.

Confirm qualifications, licensing, and insurance

Ask what licenses or registrations are required for the work in your area and verify them through the appropriate official agency. Request proof of insurance and ask what it covers. For specialized work, confirm that the person doing the work has the required qualifications. Requirements differ by location and project type, so do not assume a credential used in one state applies everywhere.

Ask for relevant experience and references

Ask whether the contractor has completed similar projects and whether you can see examples. Request references from recent customers and ask about communication, schedule changes, cleanup, and how unexpected problems were handled. Verify references independently rather than relying only on testimonials presented in a sales meeting.

Get a detailed written estimate

The estimate should describe the work, materials, quantities or specifications, labor, exclusions, cleanup, and likely schedule. Ask what is fixed and what is an allowance that could change. Give each contractor the same scope so the bids can be compared fairly. If one proposal is much cheaper, ask what it excludes or assumes rather than comparing only the final number.

Clarify permits and inspections

Ask who will apply for permits, pay fees, schedule inspections, and provide final documentation. Verify local requirements with the building department if anything is unclear. A contractor’s verbal statement that no permit is required should not replace checking the rule for your project. Keep copies of permits and inspection results with the contract.

Understand the contract and change-order process

The written agreement should identify the parties, scope, materials, total price or pricing method, payment schedule, approximate start and completion dates, cleanup, warranties, and dispute process. It should also explain how changes are requested, priced, and approved. When hidden conditions require additional work, ask for the proposed cost and schedule effect in writing before authorizing the change where practical.

Agree on payment milestones

Payment terms should be written and consistent with local requirements. Ask what work or materials correspond to each payment and what documentation you will receive. Be cautious of high-pressure tactics, unclear cash requests, or demands that do not match the contract. Avoid paying the entire amount upfront without understanding the risks and protections that apply.

Ask who will actually perform the work

Clarify whether the contractor uses employees or subcontractors, who supervises the job, who will be your day-to-day contact, and how the worksite will be secured. Ask how dust, noise, pets, children, parking, access, and waste disposal will be managed. Agree on working hours and the process for reporting concerns.

Discuss warranties, delays, and completion

Ask what workmanship and manufacturer warranties apply, who handles warranty claims, and how service requests are submitted. Discuss likely causes of delay, material availability, weather dependencies, and how schedule changes will be communicated. Define what counts as completion and whether a final walkthrough or punch list will be used. Keep records of communications, payments, photos, and approvals.

Watch for warning signs

Be cautious of pressure to sign immediately, promises that sound guaranteed, missing business details, reluctance to provide a written estimate, unexplained price changes, or requests to skip required permits. Do not let a sales deadline replace your own review. If the project is large or the agreement is complex, consider qualified independent advice before signing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get multiple contractor bids?

For substantial work, comparing multiple detailed bids can help you understand scope, pricing, and assumptions.

Can I verify a license online?

Many jurisdictions provide official license or registration lookup tools. Use the relevant state or local agency rather than an unverified third-party claim.

What if the project scope changes?

Request a written change order explaining the added work, price, and schedule effect before approving it where practical.
