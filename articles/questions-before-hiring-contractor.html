Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Home Improvement Contractor

Use these questions to compare contractor bids, check licensing and insurance, understand permits, warranties, payments, and change orders.

Choosing a contractor is about more than price. A clear scope, appropriate qualifications, reliable communication, and a written agreement can reduce misunderstandings during a home project. Requirements differ by state and locality, so use this checklist alongside official local guidance and take time to review documents before committing.

What exactly is included in the scope?

Ask the contractor to describe the work, materials, brands or specifications, quantities, preparation, cleanup, and items excluded from the price. Clarify who moves furniture, protects floors, disposes of debris, and repairs surfaces disturbed by the work. If a finish or product is important, list it in writing rather than relying on a verbal description.

Can you provide an itemized estimate?

An estimate should make it possible to understand major cost categories and allowances. Ask which costs may change and what assumptions the price relies on. Compare estimates built from the same scope; otherwise, a lower bid may simply include less work. Ask how unexpected conditions will be documented and priced before extra work begins.

Are you licensed and insured where required?

Licensing rules vary by trade and jurisdiction. Check requirements with the relevant state or local authority, and verify license status where a public lookup is available. Ask for proof of insurance appropriate to the work and confirm the details if necessary. Do not assume a license in one area covers every trade or location.

Can I contact recent references?

Ask for references for projects similar in scope and age to your home. Useful questions include whether the project stayed close to the written scope, how changes were handled, whether the site was kept safe, and how warranty issues were resolved. Reviews can provide context but should not replace independent verification and written terms.

Who handles permits and inspections?

Ask whether permits are required, who applies for them, who pays fees, and who schedules inspections. Confirm requirements with the local building department when uncertain. Keep copies of permits and final approvals. Avoid proceeding on a vague promise that approvals will be sorted out later.

What warranties apply?

Distinguish product warranties from workmanship warranties and any roof, waterproofing, or structural coverage. Ask how long coverage lasts, what is excluded, who performs repairs, and how to submit a claim. Obtain the terms in writing and keep product documentation, invoices, and completion records.

What is the payment schedule?

Ask for milestones, due dates, accepted payment methods, and any deposits required. Confirm that the schedule complies with local rules and matches the written contract. Be cautious of pressure to pay the full amount upfront or of requests that differ from the agreement. Keep receipts and records for every payment.

How are changes and delays handled?

Projects can uncover hidden conditions or face material delays. The agreement should explain how change orders are approved, how costs and dates are updated, and how disputes are handled. Ask who will be your day-to-day contact and how often you will receive progress updates. Do not rely on an informal conversation for a major change.

What is the expected timeline and site plan?

Ask for an estimated start window, work sequence, working hours, access needs, cleanup, and protection for occupants and pets. Timelines may depend on inspections, weather, materials, or other trades, so ask what could cause delays. Clarify what happens if the project is paused or the contractor cannot continue.

Watch for pressure and unclear promises

Be cautious of high-pressure deadlines, vague one-page contracts, unexplained cash-only demands, unusually large upfront payments, or guaranteed savings without assumptions. Verify any claims about rebates or tax benefits through official sources. A trustworthy professional should allow time to read the agreement and answer reasonable questions.

Keep a project file

Store the signed contract, estimates, insurance details, permits, change orders, receipts, photos, and warranty information together. Document important conversations in writing. If a dispute arises, review the contract and contact the appropriate local consumer-protection or licensing authority for guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Should I get multiple bids?

For substantial work, comparing multiple bids with the same written scope can help you understand price and approach differences.

Can I hire an unlicensed contractor?

Requirements vary by location and type of work. Verify the applicable rules and do not use unqualified workers for regulated or safety-critical tasks.

What should I do if a contractor pressures me to sign immediately?

Pause, read the contract, verify claims, and seek independent advice if needed. Do not let a deadline replace due diligence.
